The Work Others Turn Away

Replica & Specialty Bench Work

Most watchmakers refuse to touch replica and homage watches. I don't. A rep with a well-serviced movement running Swiss oils is a different watch — quieter, more accurate, and built to last years instead of months.

01

Mail-In Evaluation

$50 — you cover shipping both ways

Ship your watch in and I'll open it up and find out what's actually going on. If it's just something loose or out of place, the fix is included in the eval. If parts are broken, you get a documented, photographed diagnosis and a quote before anything else happens. Rep movements are inexpensive — worst case, a complete movement replacement runs around $200 (super clones aside), but it rarely gets that far.

02

Full Service, Swiss Materials

$250 time-only · $300 chronograph

Factories ship reps with cheap oils — over-applied, sometimes with machine debris still in the gear train. A full service means every single part comes out, gets cleaned ultrasonically in L&R solutions, and goes back together with Swiss Moebius lubricants applied jewel by jewel. The movement runs quieter, keeps better time, and won't need attention for around 7 years. Others charge $400+ for the same work.

03

Water-Resistance Service

$50 — seal service + pressure test

The only things keeping your movement dry are the gaskets and their lubrication. I remove the movement first (so a leak can't damage it), service the seals on the case back, crown, and crown tube — replacing any that are hardened, punctured, or the wrong size — then pressure-test the sealed case to 6 bar (60 m). Honest expectations: this is splash and maybe pool-level protection, never diving.

Why so reasonable? This is a passion, not a storefront. Pricing covers the expensive part — L&R cleaning solutions and Moebius oils — and leaves a little for the next tool on the bench. Every job is recorded, so you see exactly what was found and what was done.
Straight Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you work on replica and homage watches?

Yes — it's a specialty. Most watchmakers refuse them; I treat them like any other mechanical watch. Mail it in, pay $50 for the evaluation plus shipping, and you'll get a photographed diagnosis. If it's just something loose, the fix is included in the eval fee.

What exactly does a "full service" include?

A full service is a complete teardown — every single part comes out of the watch. Parts are cleaned in an ultrasonic bath with professional L&R cleaning solutions, then the movement is reassembled with Swiss Moebius oils and greases applied to specific jewels, pivots, and gears. Finished with demagnetization and multi-position regulation on the timegrapher.

$200 for time-only movements, $250 for chronographs, $300 for premium calibers (324 / 330 class). These oils last about 7 years before the next service is due. Comparable work elsewhere runs $400+.

My watch runs — do I even need a service?

It's optional, and I'll tell you honestly if yours can wait. But factories — especially rep factories — use cheap oils, over-oil the movements, and sometimes leave machine debris in the gear train. A proper service noticeably quiets the movement, improves timekeeping, and protects it for years. If your watch is running slow, I can regulate it for $50, but without finding the underlying cause it can be a losing battle — a teardown finds the real problem.

How does the water-resistance service work?

Lubrication and seals are the only two things keeping your movement dry. Rubber gaskets harden and get brittle over time — if a seal is broken, punctured, hardened, or the wrong size, water gets in.

For $50, I remove the movement first (so if the case leaks under test, there's nothing inside to ruin), then remove, lubricate, and re-seat the gaskets on the case back, crown, and crown tube — replacing any that are past saving — and pressure-test the sealed case to 6 bar (60 m) to confirm the seals hold.

So my watch will be waterproof after testing?

No — and anyone who promises otherwise is overselling. Even a watch that passes at 6 bar is not meant for swimming or diving; think rain and hand-washing, maybe a pool at best. And if the case itself leaks due to a manufacturing defect, there's no fix that wouldn't cost more than the watch. The seal service keeps an honest watch honest — it doesn't turn it into a diver.

What if something is broken — what will parts cost?

Parts are always quoted separately and you approve them before any work continues. Every step is recorded, so you'll see exactly what's broken. Rep movement parts are generally inexpensive — a complete movement replacement worst-case runs around $200 (super clones can be more), but most problems turn out to be far smaller.

My rotor is loud — can you fix that?

Rotor silencing is a quick job — remove the rotor, lubricate it properly, and reinstall. $30. If I spot obvious debris while I'm in there through the case back, I'll clean what's visible; a true cleaning of the whole movement is what the full service is for.

How does shipping work?

You mail the watch to me in Gainesville, Virginia and cover shipping both ways — I recommend insured, tracked shipping. Once I've received it, you'll get the evaluation results with photos before anything is done. Nationwide mail-in service; international by arrangement.
